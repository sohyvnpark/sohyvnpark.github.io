    Infotainment Experience(ccNC Operation)

    My role

    Infotainment UI Design, Global Market Research, GUI Guideline Update

    Client

    Hyundai MOBIS

    Tools

    Figma, Adobe Photoshop, Adobe Illustrator, Microsoft PowerPoint

    Duration

    5 months

    Overview

    'ccNC' is an infotainment system designed to enhance the Hyundai Motor Group's in-vehicle user experience.

    Hyundai MOBIS is a leading global automotive supplier specialising in advanced vehicle technologies, including autonomous driving, electrification, infotainment, and safety systems. Our team was responsible for visualising and enhancing the in-vehicle infotainment system (ccNC) for Hyundai and Kia's current and future models.

    Goal

    Result

    Incorporate 10+ features unique to each upcoming vehicle.

    Developed a novel design system and reduced design iteration time by 20%.

    Ensure visual consistency of automotive functions across sedans, SUVs, and trucks.

    Successfully designed and updated 50+ high-fidelity screens within the timeline.

    Deliver fully refined UI and GUI Guidelines within a 2-week timeline per vehicle.

    Implemented multilingual support for smooth adaptation across 5 different countries.

    How the project progressed

    Our way to solve the problem

    01

    Understand

    Fully understand the GUI guidelines and identify inconsistencies to ensure new features integrate seamlessly into the infotainment design.

    02

    Define

    Develop UI components and layouts for new vehicle features while updating and refining the GUI guidelines.

    03

    Finalise

    Incorporate final feedback into the final infotainment design and deliver it to Hyundai MOBIS for development and integration into new vehicles.

    The problems I found

    GUI Guideline causing problems

    One of the biggest challenges was ensuring that programmers and engineers could accurately follow the GUI Guidelines. My role was to bridge this gap by ensuring that all newly updated features were correctly positioned and fully aligned with the GUI Guidelines across upcoming vehicle models.

    The major solution

    Consistency in Resources

    As the vehicles neared release, maintaining design consistency between Hyundai and Kia was crucial. While both brands offered the same services and layout, their design identities were distinct. Preserving these unique brand elements while ensuring a cohesive experience was essential. Additionally, maintaining visual consistency across graphic resources was important, as they shared key functions but differed in design.

    Improving accessibility

    Internationalisation across various vehicles

    As Hyundai and Kia reached over 7.3 million users globally, internalisation became essential to delivering a consistent yet brand-specific experience. While both brands shared core services and layouts, I adapted the design system to reflect their distinct visual languages. This process deepened my understanding of each brand’s identity and ensured functional consistency without compromising usability or scalability across global markets.

    Improving accessibility

    Designing different functions

    With active communication with designers and engineers at Hyundai MOBIS, I mainly focused on design features such as the Remote Smart Parking Assistant(RSPA), Split Screens, and My Vehicle while creating accurate GUI guidelines.

    My key decisions

    The calls that shaped the work

    Three decisions that kept a growing, multi-brand infotainment system consistent and buildable.

    01

    Treat the guideline as the source of truth

    Challenge
    Engineers struggled to follow the GUI guidelines accurately as new features kept arriving.
    My decision
    I placed every new feature against the guideline and updated the guideline alongside the screens, instead of designing screens first.
    Impact
    Clearer handover to development and a design system that cut iteration time by 20%.
    02

    One system, two brands

    Challenge
    Hyundai and Kia share the same services and layouts, but each has its own design identity.
    My decision
    I kept layouts and components shared, and let each brand speak through its shapes: sharp, angular corners for Kia, echoing the edges of its logo, and soft, rounded corners for Hyundai's more classic, timeless look.
    Impact
    Consistent functions across sedans, SUVs and trucks, with 50+ high-fidelity screens delivered on time.
    03

    Design for every language from the start

    Challenge
    With over 7.3 million users worldwide, screens had to work in many languages without breaking.
    My decision
    I adapted the design system so layouts held up across languages such as Korean, English and Japanese.
    Impact
    Multilingual support rolled out smoothly across 5 countries.

    My learnings &
    what can I do better next time?

    1. Mobile UX/UI vs. automotive UX/UI

    Automotive UX/UI design prioritises minimising driver distraction, requiring glanceable, intuitive interfaces. I focused on organising information effectively with clear typography, optimised layouts, and simplified visuals to enhance readability while driving.

    2. Maintaining consistency across vehicle models

    A key challenge was ensuring design consistency across Hyundai and Kia’s infotainment systems while preserving each brand’s unique identity. Balancing uniformity and differentiation was crucial for a seamless user experience.

    Moving forward, I aim to collaborate more closely with developers to refine design-to-development workflows and conduct usability validations to better align with real user needs. This project strengthened my passion for user-centric automotive UX/UI design, and I’m eager to build more accessible and engaging in-vehicle experiences.

    Technology into Reality(Noveto x Blink)

    My role

    Video Edit, Motion Design, UI Design, Technology Research, Footage Search, Sound Level Adjustment

    Client

    Hyundai Motor Group (B2B)

    Tools

    Adobe After Effects, Adobe Photoshop, Figma, Microsoft Excel

    Duration

    2 months

    Overview

    Noveto X Blink's innovative technology was introduced at the Hyundai Motor Group's 2024 Open Innovation Lounge.

    OI Lounge is an annual event that showcases Hyundai Motor Group's dedication to fostering innovation through collaboration with global startups. I was tasked with creating a video explaining Noveto X Blink's PoC (Proof of Concept) technologies - 'Personalised Speaker with Audio-Zone Separation' and 'Face-Tracking'.

    Goal

    Result

    Translate the given user scenario into a visual narrative within a two-week timeline.

    100% scenario-to-visual alignment within the two-week timeline, reducing revision cycles by 50%.

    Create 3 infotainment screens with motion graphics showcasing the technology.

    Designed and animated 3 infotainment screens improving clarity and engagement.

    Optimise audio levels and spatial sound design to clearly demonstrate the audio-zone separation technology.

    Enhanced the understanding of audio-zone separation technology by 10%.

    The technology

    At the 2024 OI Lounge

    Noveto X Blink presented a novel entertainment technology that delivers personalised sound experiences based on passenger positioning. This technology integrates facial tracking via RGB cameras, combined with beamforming technology to precisely direct and isolate sound to specific areas, creating an immersive audio experience.

    My key decisions

    The calls that shaped the work

    Three decisions that made complex audio technology easy to understand in a short video.

    01

    Story first, screens second

    Challenge
    Face tracking and beamforming are hard to explain, and the timeline was only two weeks.
    My decision
    I turned the user scenario into a clear visual story before designing any screens.
    Impact
    100% scenario-to-visual alignment and 50% fewer revision cycles.
    02

    Make sound visible

    Challenge
    Audio-zone separation is invisible, and easy to miss in a video.
    My decision
    I visualised each passenger's sound zone in the UI and motion, and balanced the audio levels so viewers could hear the separation.
    Impact
    A 10% better understanding of the audio-zone technology.
    03

    UI designed for the camera

    Challenge
    Infotainment screens shown on video are seen briefly and from a distance.
    My decision
    I simplified three screens, with clear layouts and motion that guides the eye to what matters.
    Impact
    Screens that read clearly on camera and kept viewers engaged.

    My key learnings from this project:

    1. Designing infotainment UI for visual storytelling

    Learned how to adapt infotainment UI for video, ensuring clear layouts, animations, and interactions that effectively showcase the technology. Next time, I would focus on refining micro-interactions and transitions to enhance realism and engagement.

    2. Translating complex technology into intuitive UX video

    Gained experience in simplifying advanced features like facial tracking and beamforming into user-friendly, visually engaging designs. Moving forward, I aim to conduct more benchmarking on infotainment UI trends to improve clarity and usability.

    3. Collaboration & iteration for clarity

    Improved communication with clients and cross-functional teams to refine designs based on feedback, ensuring consistency and usability across infotainment screens. In future projects, I would establish a more structured feedback loop to streamline revisions and enhance efficiency.

    Beyond the Windshield(LG Electronics)

    My role

    UI Design, AI-assisted Motion & Playlist Visuals, Cross-team Collaboration

    Client

    LG Electronics
    (Vehicle Solution)

    Tools

    Figma, Midjourney

    Recognition

    PACE Pilot Award Finalist
    (2026 Automotive News)

    Overview

    The right information, in the right place, at the right time.

    Today's cockpits spread information across many screens, pulling drivers' eyes away from the road. UltraView brings it onto the windshield instead: a compact AR-HUD places driving-critical cues like speed, ADAS alerts and navigation far out on the road, while LG's Hover Screen shows navigation, vehicle status, HVAC and media on a large virtual screen about 1.2 m away.

    Each piece of information sits at its own distance, so drivers move their eyes less and understand the road faster.

    Context-aware welcoming

    A welcome that knows where you're headed

    As the drive begins, welcome content and a music suggestion rise onto the AR-HUD, while the CID shows themed playlists for the journey. I designed this moment with AI, working closely with our UI design team and LG's product managers.

    A music suggestion rises onto the AR-HUD, with AI-themed playlists on the CID
    *Video from LG Electronics' official materials.

    01

    AI-generated motion & playlist visuals

    Created the motion of the welcome content rising onto the AR-HUD and the themed playlist visuals on the CID with AI, refining each theme together with the product manager so the journey feels personal from the very first moment.

    02

    In-vehicle UI screens

    Designed most of the cockpit UI, from the widget bar (Navigation, Weather, Music, Dashboard, Mail) to the song recommendations, driving view and climate controls, keeping every screen consistent and glanceable with our UI team and LG's product managers.

    AI motion process

    Making the welcoming motion with AI

    How I created the AI video resources for the context-aware welcoming, from the first theme ideas to the motion rising onto the AR-HUD and the playlist visuals on the CID. AI sped up exploration, while every step was shaped through conversations with the product manager and our UI team.

    1. 01

      Align

      Defined the welcoming scenario and playlist themes with LG's product manager.

    2. 02

      Explore with AI

      Generated first theme visuals and key frames in Midjourney to explore moods quickly.

    3. 03

      Prompt study

      Studied how wording, style references and parameters changed the results, and built a prompt set that kept every theme consistent.

    4. 04

      Animate & refine

      Turned the visuals into the rising AR-HUD motion and iterated on the product manager's feedback.

    5. 05

      Integrate

      Fitted the motion and playlists into the AR-HUD and CID screens with our UI design team.

    My key decisions

    The calls that shaped the work

    Three decisions behind the welcoming moment and the screens around it.

    01

    Short on the HUD, rich on the CID

    Challenge
    Anything on the windshield competes with the road for the driver's attention.
    My decision
    I kept the AR-HUD to a single, glanceable suggestion and left browsing to the themed playlists on the CID.
    Impact
    A welcome that feels personal without pulling the driver's eyes off the road.
    02

    A prompt set for consistent AI visuals

    Challenge
    Early AI results looked different every time, so the themes didn't feel like one family.
    My decision
    I studied how prompts behave and built a reusable prompt set before producing the final visuals.
    Impact
    Consistent themes, and changes from the product manager's feedback in hours rather than days.
    03

    One visual language across every screen

    Challenge
    The cockpit mixes widgets, recommendations, driving info and climate controls.
    My decision
    I applied the same components and visual rules to every screen, from the widget bar to the climate controls.
    Impact
    A coherent, glanceable cockpit, part of a project named a 2026 PACE Pilot Award finalist.

    My key learnings from this project:

    1. Designing for different distances

    Placing content on the AR-HUD, the Hover Screen or the CID changes how quickly a driver can take it in. I learned to decide not just how a screen looks, but where each piece of information should live.

    2. AI as part of the design process

    Using AI for motion and playlist visuals let me explore themes quickly and react to the product manager's feedback in hours rather than days, while keeping the final quality in my hands.

    3. Collaboration across teams

    Working between our UI design team and LG's product managers taught me to align early, explain my decisions clearly and keep everyone moving towards the same story.

    Seeing UltraView named a finalist for the 2026 Automotive News PACE Pilot Award made me incredibly proud of what our team built together.

    My role

    UX Research (User Interview/Testing, Data/Competitor Analysis), UI/UX Design, High-Fidelity Prototyping, Team Collaboration

    Client

    Confidential
    (Automotive Industry - B2B)

    Tools

    Figma, ProtoPie, Adobe Photoshop, Microsoft Teams, Microsoft Excel, Google Forms, Google Analytics

    Duration

    4 months

    The automotive industry is focusing on delivering personalised & real-time curated information to enhance users' in-vehicle experience.

    Goal

    Result

    Design and develop a unique infotainment app within 4 months.

    Successfully conducted 7 user interview sessions (2 UPD & 5 IDI) within the timeline.

    Enhance in-vehicle location discovery and media exploration experiences for the client.

    Achieved a 20% improvement in infotainment usability experience.

    Project mission

    Creating a 'unique' and 'new' in-vehicle experience

    Working with a major client in the automotive industry, our team needed to fully grasp the essence of their project. Below is a diagram I created to illustrate the experience they aimed to achieve.

    Destination (Places to visit) Curation

    Expected Target Users:

    • Users who want an easy comparison of locations.
    • Users interested in discovering new places.

    Expected Impact:

    • Provide tailored POI information relevant to the user's context.
    • Reducing decision-making and exploration time for destinations.

    Content (Media) Curation

    Expected Target Users:

    • Users who want context-driven, differentiated experiences.
    • Users who need media recommendations based on their preferences.

    Expected Impact:

    • Deliver immersive media experiences with context-driven content.
    • Improve content accessibility through personalised curation

    = How can we make 'curation' actually useful?

    The solution - Destination curation

    Real-time and personalised in-vehicle experience

    We aimed to turn the uncertainty drivers feel at the start of their journey into a confident, exciting experience by offering real-time, personalised location and media recommendations. The service aimed to deliver unique in-vehicle experiences tailored to each user.

    01

    Real-time based trending information

    Collects high-demand information based on the user's current location and provides real-time data to the user.

    02

    Customised recommendations based on driving situation

    Gathers information about the user's current driving situation and provides real-time data that suits the specific circumstances.

    03

    Personalised experiences based on preferences

    Recommends tailored information based on the user's past records and preferences, suggesting content that matches their tastes.

    Desk research & competitive analysis - Destination curation

    The increasing user needs for a personalised experience

    The automotive industry is currently widely using data analysis and AI technology to provide personalised services. Beyond in-vehicle customisation, it analyses individual preferences and driving habits to develop more refined recommendation systems.

    User research - Destination curation

    Users wanted locations recommended based on their preference

    Our team conducted an online survey with 100 vehicle owners aged 20 to 60 and user interviews with 10 users from the online survey. to understand users' in-vehicle information exploration patterns and preferences. The goal was to establish the direction for information recommendations and prioritise items based on users' needs.

    Online Survey

    • Users prioritise distance > parking availability > congestion > time of day when exploring locations.
    • Users prefer recommendations based on keywords, internal behaviour patterns, and external behaviour patterns.

    User Interviews

    • High demand for filtering and sorting options to refine results.
    • Desire for recommendations based on previous visits or preferences.
    • Trust in ratings and visit frequency as reliability indicators.
    Insight 1

    Provide personalised curation information based on keywords, internal behaviour patterns, and external behaviour patterns.

    Insight 2

    Prioritise providing information on distance, parking availability, congestion, and the number of visitors.

    Insight 3

    Recommend optimised locations by combining condition values.

    My key decisions

    The calls that shaped the work

    Three decisions that turned a broad brief for 'unique' curation into something drivers actually found useful.

    01

    Research before features

    Challenge
    The client wanted a 'unique and new' curation service, but it was unclear what drivers would really use.
    My decision
    We surveyed 100 vehicle owners and interviewed 10 of them before defining any features.
    Impact
    A clear priority order (distance, parking, congestion, time of day) that set the information shown on every card.
    02

    Test with a prototype that feels real

    Challenge
    Static screens couldn't show how drivers would actually browse, filter and react to recommendations.
    My decision
    I built the high-fidelity prototype in ProtoPie, with real interactions, and used it for user testing.
    Impact
    Testing felt far more natural, so participants reacted as they would in the car and gave more realistic feedback.
    03

    Make personalisation explainable

    Challenge
    Users wanted recommendations based on their preferences, plus control over the results.
    My decision
    I showed why each place is recommended and added filters and sorting, so suggestions never feel random.
    Impact
    A 20% improvement in infotainment usability.

    My key learnings from this project:

    1. Designing with intention

    The importance of trying to think intentionally about every element of the project and how it can contribute to the end result.

    2. Crafting effective user interviews

    Crafting user interview questions to engage effectively with users and gather insightful responses.

    3. Turning insights into design decisions

    The importance of translating user insights into actionable design decisions while aligning stakeholder expectations for a more effective and user-centred outcome.

    Designing with AI(Vibe-coding)

    My role

    Design direction, UX/UI design, Motion, Design QA

    Made with

    Claude (Anthropic)

    Stack

    HTML, CSS, JavaScript, GitHub Pages

    Versions

    470+ published

    Overview

    My portfolio, designed in conversation with Claude.

    I wanted a site that feels like my own design work, not a template. Instead of building it line by line on my own, I paired with Claude: I set the direction, reviewed every build and refined the details until they felt right.

    Claude took on most of the heavy lifting in code, while I read through it, adjusted it and kept every design decision in my hands.

    How we worked

    Say, build, check, refine

    The same loop, more than 470 times, from the first layout to the last pixel.

    01

    Say

    I described what I wanted in plain words, often with a reference from Pinterest or a quick sketch.

    02

    Build

    Claude drafted the HTML, CSS and JavaScript and published a preview within minutes, and I stepped into the code where needed.

    03

    Check

    I review it like design QA, every day, on desktop and on my phone, hunting for lag and bugs with screenshots and screen recordings.

    04

    Refine

    I pointed out what felt off, from a menu that jumped to a name that flickered, and we went again.

    Versions

    From a template look to my own

    The home screen is the first thing people see, so it changed the most. Each version came from looking at the last one and asking what still didn't feel like me.

    Design system

    A small system, used everywhere

    I set up the site the way I set up design systems at work: a few colours, two typefaces and a handful of components, reused on every page so new sections feel consistent from day one.

    Pixel horse

    How the horse came to life

    I wanted one graphic that could carry the whole site. We built a galloping horse and jockey out of simple shapes, then turned it into dots, a dot language that now runs through the buttons, the menu and the ending.

    From shape to signature

    • ShapeBuilt from simple ellipses and capsules. I directed the anatomy: a lean thoroughbred, neck reaching forward, jockey crouched low.
    • MotionEach leg runs its own phase of the gallop, while the mane and tail wave with every stride. Stepped frames give it a film-like rhythm.
    • DotsThe shape is sampled on a grid and drawn as round dots in a soft blue, so it reads as a pixel graphic.
    • SystemIt shrinks into tiny dot horses for the buttons and menu, slows down when you hover, and only draws when you can see it.

    Prompts

    I wasn't good at it from the start

    My first prompts were vague, and so were the results. I learned along the way by watching YouTube tutorials and how other people vibe code. Slowly my prompts turned into proper design feedback: what is wrong, where it is, and what “done” looks like.

    At first

    Make the title a bit cooler. Something unique, like my own website.

    Something feels off. Can you make it look nicer?

    Later

    The S looks like it's sticking out. Align the letters with the left edge of BACK.▶ Screen recording attached

    Going from the lab to the case study, the light home screen flashes for a split second.▶ Screen recording attached

    On mobile, keep the text block squared. Make the font smaller instead of switching to left-aligned.

    Trial and error

    What went wrong, and what it taught me

    Many versions never made it past a first look. Each miss was frustrating in the moment, but it changed how I brief, review and work with AI.

    01

    Seven tries for one page

    What happened
    S(AI) Lab went through a grid, dark mode, folders, an archive, a 3D card stack and a scrolling reel before the floating letters.
    What I learned
    A good reference beats a long description, and dropping a finished-looking version is part of designing.
    02

    One fix, a new bug

    What happened
    Letters overlapped, ran off-screen and slid under the header. Patching one problem kept causing the next.
    What I learned
    Describe the rules, not the symptom: stay inside the page, avoid the text, spring back home.
    03

    It looked fine on my laptop

    What happened
    On my phone the site lagged, and a light screen flashed for a split second between pages.
    What I learned
    Test on real devices every day, and record the screen. Some bugs only show up in slow motion.

    My key decisions

    The calls that shaped the site

    Claude and I built the site together, but the direction was always mine. These are the calls that shaped how it looks, feels and performs, and the reasons behind them.

    01

    Personality, not decoration

    Challenge
    Automotive portfolios often look alike.
    My decision
    A pixel horse made of dots became my signature, galloping in the hero, on buttons and in the ending.
    Impact
    A site people remember, while the work still comes first.
    02

    The details are the design

    Challenge
    AI gets things 90% right. The last 10% decides the quality.
    My decision
    I flagged every small flaw from recordings: a header shifting by a few pixels, a twitching image, a menu that jumped.
    Impact
    Polish that holds up on desktop and on the phone.
    03

    Own the whole experience

    Challenge
    A beautiful site that lags loses trust.
    My decision
    I treated performance as design: 6 fonts down to 2, and animations that only draw when you can see them.
    Impact
    A faster first load and smoother scrolling on mobile.

    My key learnings from this project:

    1. Clear words are a design tool

    The more precisely I described a problem, the better the result. Writing good feedback for AI is the same skill as writing it for a developer.

    2. Thinking in code constraints

    Seeing how each idea turned into code taught me what is easy or costly to build, which makes me a better partner for engineering teams.

    3. AI speeds up making, not judging

    Claude made it possible to try ideas in minutes, but every call on taste, hierarchy and quality stayed with me.

    This site is still a work in progress. I check it on desktop and mobile every day, and keep refining it.
